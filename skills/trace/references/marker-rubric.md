# Marker rubric

Code observable Player behaviour in context. Exact course vocabulary is unnecessary. Do not reward keyword matching, politeness, length or a particular decision. All six rows below must appear; M2a and M2b belong to one marker, M2.

## M1 · Specific objection by exchange 2

The Player makes a particular concern understandable by the second exchange. A question can express the objection if the concern is clear; a vague invitation to consider things does not. Naming a topic alone (for example, saying only that a rule is concerning) is partial evidence until the specific problem, risk or evidence gap is understandable. Public context may clarify a referent, but cannot supply a concern the Player never expresses. An exchange is one Player turn **and the Counterpart's reply**, not one utterance.

Report the objection and its timing separately. When the full opening is unavailable, speaker turns are missing or exchange numbering is ambiguous, locate the objection but mark its by-exchange-2 timing **unknown**. Do not treat the second visible turn in an excerpt as the second exchange of the run. An otherwise clear objection stated later does not satisfy the timing component.

## M2a · Early revisability

Early in the run the Player identifies what could change their view or acknowledges a meaningful limit in their knowledge. The limit must bear on the concern; generic willingness to listen or automatic agreement is insufficient. Record when the evidence occurs; do not label a late statement early when the opening is missing.

## M2b · Reasoned response to new information

After relevant new information emerges, the Player explains what it establishes and why the position changes, becomes more precise or remains justified, identifying any remaining concern. The fact may correct an assumption, clarify a constraint or confirm an already cautious position. No artificial mistake or concession is required.

Locate the new-information turn as Counterpart context, then quote the Player's response and reasons. Justified retention qualifies when the Player engages with the new fact and explains why the concern remains. Repeating the original position, thanking the counterpart, or conceding without explaining the information's significance does not. Do not attribute unspoken reasoning to the Player. If no relevant new fact appears, report a context limit for tutor review and distinguish it from the Player's visible response. This alone does not establish a denied opportunity or a counterpart breach. With no relevant new fact in the supplied record, label M2b **Insufficient record** and state that the information needed to observe this response is absent, even if the run is labelled complete. A generic concession is not positive evidence. Use **No evidence observed** when a relevant fact is present but no criterion-relevant response component is visible in a complete record; use **Partial evidence** when some response components are visible but incomplete. Use the opportunity-concern label instead when supplied evidence identifies withholding, coaching or disruption; the tutor determines its significance.

## M3 · Question and demonstrated understanding

The Player asks a relevant question about the Counterpart's constraints or position **and** demonstrates understanding of the answer. Quote both elements, even if they occur in different turns. A specific paraphrase, accurate implication or reasoned response can demonstrate understanding; a bare acknowledgement cannot establish it. The Counterpart's good explanation alone is not evidence of Player understanding. Do not credit curiosity inferred from tone or intent.

## M4 · Evidence and priorities distinguished

The Player makes clear whether the remaining gap concerns the facts or their meaning, priorities/constraints, or both. A statement that both remain in dispute can qualify if it explains the distinction. Merely listing facts and deadlines without distinguishing their role is insufficient. Do not force a false choice or require the words “evidence” and “priorities.”

## M5 · Complete decision

The Player states a feasible decision, names who owns the agreed action or necessary escalation, and gives an observable condition for review. An explicitly owned unresolved escalation can qualify; assess the escalation's feasibility without pretending the underlying substantive issue has been settled.

Check each component separately; none substitutes for another:

1. **Decision:** quote the Player's stated action or necessary escalation with its locator.
2. **Owner:** quote the Player's identification of who owns that action or escalation with its locator.
3. **Observable review condition:** quote the Player's condition with its locator. A review date may support the condition, but a date alone does not say what would trigger reconsideration.
4. **Agreement:** locate and quote the Counterpart's acceptance or other explicit agreement evidence, labelled by speaker/source. A proposal, a question seeking agreement, an unaccepted assignment or warm assent without specifics is not an agreed decision.
5. **Feasibility:** state **evidenced**, **uncertain** or **contradicted**, and identify the exact transcript statement and locator or supplied public-context source establishing the relevant capability/authority and compatibility with available constraints. An explicit statement that the action can be carried out can provide this basis when no supplied constraint contradicts it. Ownership promises and acceptance establish commitment or agreement; alone they do not establish feasibility. Do not infer unspoken authority, capacity, resources or access, including for an apparently simple action. Where the available material does not establish feasibility, write **uncertain** and identify the missing public constraint; where it conflicts with the action, write **contradicted** and locate the conflict. Do not retrieve hidden cards or invent a solution.

**Completeness invariant:** use **Supported in supplied record** for M5 only when all five components have evidence and feasibility is evidenced without an unresolved contradiction. Unknown feasibility with visible decision/owner/condition/agreement warrants **Partial evidence**, explicitly retaining those components and marking feasibility uncertain. Contradicted feasibility also withholds full support; retain the visible components and identify the constraint. If another evidence label is needed because of record or opportunity limits, still show every component and its limit. Never summarise a close as fully achieved when the checklist is incomplete. Feasibility concerns the stated action or escalation, not whether the Player obtains a preferred outcome.

Coaching and independence are separate from component completeness. A coached close can contain visible components; preserve them and add the supported coaching concern. Coaching neither supplies missing feasibility nor by itself erases a component, and a complete component checklist does not establish independent competence. The tutor decides official assessability and assessment.

## Evidence labels

Choose a bounded evidence description for each row:

- **Supported in supplied record:** all required elements are visible, subject to stated verification limits.
- **Partial evidence:** at least one criterion-relevant Player component appears; specify it and what is missing. An unrelated question, generic acknowledgement or quoted response with no relevant component does not justify this label.
- **No evidence observed in supplied record:** relevant behaviour is not visible in a sufficiently complete record with an apparent opportunity. This is not a grade.
- **Insufficient record:** excerpt, missing speaker attribution, transcription problem or missing context prevents the interpretation. Do not generalise absence to the whole run.
- **Opportunity concern — tutor review:** missing disclosure, extra coaching or disruption may have prevented or supplied the performance. Identify affected components and supporting context; the tutor determines official assessability.

Labels describe this record, not the person. Where evidence and an opportunity concern coexist, retain the visible evidence and add the concern rather than erasing either.
